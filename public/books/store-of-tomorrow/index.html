Store of Tomorrow

By Nia Kendi · 4 chapters · Free to read

Also available: Kiswahili

For eleven years, Tunu Sengo has rolled up the metal shutters of Duka la Kesho every morning before the sun clears the tin roofs of Mavuno Street. Her customers trust her to revive phones that others wrote off, suppliers know she pays slowly but without fail, and her landlord knows she has never been more than two days late on rent. Then a sudden rumor spreads through the market: businesses held under certain residency categories are about to be shut down. There is no official notice on her door and no municipal inspector has arrived, yet customers hesitate, suppliers freeze credit, her landlord starts asking uneasy questions, and a competitor three doors down begins measuring her shopfront with hungry eyes. When Tunu visits the municipal office, she uncovers an even harder truth: there is no immediate closure, but there is a strict 21-day compliance window—and her file contains a historical registration flaw that no current receipt can paper over. To save her livelihood without becoming a victim or surrendering the shop to a nominal partner, Tunu must navigate a labyrinth of old documents, resist unlawful harassment, and prove that the business she built with her own hands can remain hers under the law.
